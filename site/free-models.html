Free AI Models You Can Use Today

You do not need to pay for AI. This chapter lists free, beginner-friendly options and explains the difference between web chatbots and local models that run on your own computer.

Option 1: Free web chatbots

Many AI companies offer a free chatbot you can use in a web browser or phone app.

Good for: trying AI, quick notes, learning how prompts work.

Watch out for: the free version may use your chats to train future models, so never paste client details. Sign in with an email you already have and do not pay unless your employer approves it.

Option 2: Local AI (runs on your computer)

A local AI model downloads to your computer or phone and runs there. Nothing leaves your device, and you do not need an internet connection after downloading the model.

Good for: privacy, practicing with de-identified examples, working offline.

Examples of free local tools (check current downloads before installing):

  • Ollama — runs free open models on your computer
  • LM Studio — a free app to download and chat with local models
  • Jan — a free local AI app for desktop

These tools use open models (such as Llama, Gemma, or Qwen) that are free to download and run. You do not need an API key, and you do not need to create an account.

Option 3: Free tiers of bigger tools

Some well-known AI assistants have generous free daily limits. They are fine for learning, but again: no client details, and check whether your chats are stored or used for training.

Our recommendation for beginners

Start with a free web chatbot to practice with fake details (like the examples in this manual). Once you are comfortable, try a local model if you want more privacy or want to work offline.

What to avoid

  • Do not enter payment information unless your employer has reviewed and approved the tool.
  • Do not create accounts using a client's name or information.
  • Do not install browser extensions or apps that promise "free AI" unless you can verify who made them.
